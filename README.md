## Research method

- **Competitor data (Q4, Q5):** read from each retailer's own product or category page in Chrome on 8 Oct 2026. Where a figure could not be read on the page, the workbook says so instead of estimating.
- **Category for Q4:** office chairs, because all five retailers sell them online and they match During Days' range.
- **Kogan prices:** the non-member price is used as the selling price (anyone can pay it). Member prices are recorded separately.
- **Demand evidence (Q5):** Google Trends Australia (5-year comparison), Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026 category PDFs, ABS Household Spending Indicator, and review counts on retailer pages. Observed evidence and analyst inference are labelled separately.
- **Calculations (Q1, Q6, Q8, Q9):** done with formulas in the workbook and independently re-computed outside Excel (170 checks, no differences); the `QA` sheet repeats the key checks.

## Assumptions

- Q6 ignores GST because the assessment does not say whether $149.95 includes it. A GST sensitivity is shown separately.
- A 15% target net margin and a $15 outbound shipping cost for bulky items are my own assumptions (Q5 break-even calculator); they are marked in blue and can be changed.
- Original prices at Big W are derived from the "SAVE $x" badge on the page.

## Limitations

- Prices and promotions change often; the data is a snapshot of 8 October 2026.
- Delivery costs depend on the postcode and could not be verified, so they are not added to competitor prices.
- The 15 chairs are not identical products; comparisons are made within chair type where it matters.
- Google Trends averages were read from the chart by eye and are approximate. Trends measures search interest, not sales.
- No supplier costs were available, so profitability for the Q5 products is a target to test, not a finding.
- Australia Post's Home & Garden online spend appears as $9.9b in its own category report and as $11.4b in press coverage of the main report. The two figures are not reconciled; $9.9b is the one read directly from Australia Post's category PDF, and $11.4b comes from press coverage that was not checked against the main report.
